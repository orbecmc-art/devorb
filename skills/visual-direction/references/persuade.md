# Persuade

The visitor arrived to decide something and, ideally, act on it. Here the design is part of what they judge, so the direction may shape the whole page, as long as every part still works as a page.

## The first screen

At 1280 x 800, without scrolling, a stranger sees what this is, why it matters to them and what to do: the headline, one sentence and the primary action, plus the one thing only this business can show.

- Put the action itself on the first screen, working: whatever visitors to this kind of business came to do (choose a time, add to basket, copy the install command). A link that only jumps further down the page does not count.
- Show the real thing: the place, the product, the service at work. A screenshot of the product, captured from it, beats a drawing of a screen. With no product to capture yet, build its main screen with real controls and sample values that read as samples. Never a hand-drawn browser or phone frame, and never an image URL typed from memory.
- However far the direction goes, the page keeps working: you can tell where to click, and every line can be read.

## Sections come from the visitor's job

Name the job first, then build the sections real sites in that category are built around. The stock sequence (hero, three features, numbers, testimonials, email band) belongs to software sites; a clinic, a shop or a studio has its own.

| Job | The action, and where | Sections the category owns | How it ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| Book (clinic, restaurant, class) | Book, in the nav and the first screen, repeated beside the services | what is offered, the place, how to get there, hours | hours, address and the booking again |
| Buy (a product, a small shop) | the product with price and options | the range, the product in use, delivery and guarantee | the offer again |
| Sign up (software) | start, with its terms in one line | the product at work, a code or integration sample, pricing | the first action again |
| Hire (studio, freelancer) | a plain contact | the work itself, what they do, where | contact details |
| Visit (shop, venue, gallery) | today's hours and the address | what is on now, what is coming, getting there | visit information |
| Attend (event) | tickets | the date and place, lineup, schedule, venue | tickets again |
| Subscribe (newsletter, podcast) | the email field, often the whole first screen | a sample issue, the archive | the one job that may close on an email field |

A mailing-list close on a page whose job is not subscribing is a reflex: end on what the visitor came to do.

## Words and volume

- Keep each screenful near 80 words. Fill space with larger type and pictures, never with extra sentences.
- One primary action, worded the same in the nav, the first screen and the close, and nowhere competing with it above the fold.
- Put each piece of proof next to the claim it backs, and only proof the client gave. Without any, state plain facts or leave the claim out.
- A guarantee, free trial or "no card needed" appears only when the business offers it.

## Direction notes for Persuade

- At most one candidate may come from the tools or products the audience uses; the rest come from elsewhere in their world.
- A display face that nearly every generated page ships (the free editorial serifs and the geometric sans that models reach for by default) needs a reason written in the contract that only this brief supplies. A cliché of the trade ("a law firm wants a classic serif") is not a reason. The brief naming the face is.
- Choose light or dark by picturing the reader: where they are and in what light.
- Let one element be bold; everything else stays quiet and exact.
- Colour defaults to a complementary pair split by section (`devorb:design-system`, `references/color-finish.md`), unless the spec or `DESIGN.md` fixes one colour. The `Sections:` line says which colour owns each section, such as the primary on the light sections and the complement on the proof band.
