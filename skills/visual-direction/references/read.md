# Read

The visitor came with a question and wants to understand the answer. The page is first a document of its kind (documentation, a guide, an article, a changelog) and only then this product's.

## Frame and column

Split the page in two in the contract:

- **The frame:** everything around the text: header, navigation, page title, section headings, and the styling of code, tables and callouts. Here the direction commits fully, so the page could belong to no other product.
- **The column:** the running text. It stays calm: a text face chosen for long reading, strong contrast, lines of comfortable length, a still background. The direction never reaches into it.

Two checks on a draft. Seen at a glance, is it a document, or a poster? A poster means the direction leaked into the column: take it out of there. With the logo hidden, would a reader know whose docs these are? If not, the frame is still generic: push the direction further into it.

## Wayfinding

For the first screen, say how readers learn where they are, what title and first paragraph meet them, and how they get to the next thing (a contents list, headings, previous and next links). The reading order comes from the content, never from the direction. Navigation is the kind readers already know: a side tree, top tabs or inline contents.

## Material

- Keep monospace to code and measured values; running text and headings use the text and display faces.
- Present code, tables, callouts and figures the conventional way, dressed in the project's type and palette.
- Plan with the document's true contents, including its most awkward piece (the longest code block, the widest table), not invented filler.

## Direction notes for Read

The best candidates are printed things the audience already reads to find answers: the manuals, almanacs, catalogues and guides of their trade, and how those organise and point. Imitating a tool they operate turns a document into fancy dress; borrow from it only type, palette, density and one signature detail.
