# Size of the work and red flags

## Size the work

Once you understand what they want and they've picked an approach, say how big the work is in plain words. They can override it.

| Size | The design doc |
|------|----------------|
| A small change | A short one: what you already covered together, written down. It goes straight to the user, without the reviewer. |
| A project | The full written design, read by the reviewer before the user sees it. |

Short or full, the doc has an **Approaches** part: the two or three ways you considered building it, what each trades off, which one they picked, and why. They pick in the conversation, before you write the doc.

If it grows mid-task, stop, say so, and step up a size.

If this is really several independent projects, say so, agree on an order, and align on them one at a time.

## Red flags

| Thought | Reality |
|---------|---------|
| "I'll offer a menu of what they might want" | Options steer. Ask them to describe it first. |
| "I'll fill in sensible defaults" | Good. Say them out loud. A default they never heard is one they couldn't reject. |
| "I'll ask them where that's handled in the code" | Facts are yours. Send a subagent to find out. |
| "The code keeps records for 90 days, so that's the rule" | That number may be an accident someone typed years ago. How long to keep records is the owner's call: write it as an assumption and ask. |
| "Five questions at once will be faster" | One per message, unless they asked for a grilling round. |
| "I've answered their questions, so I'll write the doc" | Lay out the approaches first, in a message of their own (or, when nothing was open from the start, in your first message's short design). |
| "I should explain the process first" | Ask your question. The process shows itself. |
| "They said 'sounds good' to the idea" | Approval covers what you showed them. A design doc you haven't written isn't approved. |
| "The spike works, I'll keep building on it" | Keeping it is a new request. Back through the gate. |
